About

Step by step, at any scale

I grew up in a small village and learned early to move forward one step at a time. Then came Moscow, and numbers at a growing scale: a federal research center, the consolidated budget of one of the world's largest metro systems, and payroll at a state energy corporation.

Moving to the U.S. was my biggest project yet. I managed my family's immigration case, working with our attorney on documents, deadlines, and agencies, and brought my family here.

Big projects don't scare me: any scale is a sequence of clear steps. Now in South Florida, I'm doing what I've always done, learning the new system step by step and turning complex numbers into clear answers.

What I do

Budgeting & Planning

Consolidated budgets, variance analysis, scenario forecasting. Built for one of the world's largest metro systems, and explained to project owners who never opened a spreadsheet.

Payroll & Compensation

Reconciliation, deductions, and payroll reporting in SAP HCM and 1C ERP. I was the person employees called when pay didn't make sense: around 20 questions a day, every answer in plain language.

Economic Modeling

Driver-based models and forecasting methodologies from years in research. This is where my habit of testing numbers before trusting them comes from.

Regulations & Documents

Labor law, tax rules, and primary documents: I study them carefully and translate legal language into clear payroll rules, so every number has a document behind it.

Upward to executives, sideways to department heads, downward to employees: the same numbers, explained for each audience.

How I work

From numbers to answers

Same data, two languages. On the left: what the system shows. On the right: what people hear from me.

Payroll Deduction adj. −$184.20 | code 2310 | ref. 03/2026
→

“You were overpaid in March. This one-time correction lowers your April net pay by $184.20.”

Budgeting Maintenance plan 12.4M | actual 13.9M | var +12.1%
→

“Maintenance ran 12% over plan because repairs scheduled for Q3 were done in Q2: a timing shift, not new spending.”

Modeling Scenario B: ridership −8% | fare +5% → revenue −3.4%
→

“If ridership drops 8%, a 5% fare increase won't fully cover it. Revenue would still be about 3% short.”

Regulations Bonus policy §4.2 | reduction requires 30-day notice
→

“We can't reduce this quarter's bonus retroactively: the policy requires 30 days' notice. The earliest we can change it is next quarter.”
